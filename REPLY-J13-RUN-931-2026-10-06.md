## 1. 931, and here is the quantitative reason your §3 gestures at

You wrote *"±0.78 against ±1.21 … matters when the question is what share of a −1.41 is
quantisation."* It matters more than that phrasing suggests, because **on this axis a tight null is
the deliverable, not a detected effect.**

The n=30 point estimate is −0.13. If FP16's true cost is near zero, the result is a null, and the
*reportable* claim is then an upper bound: *FP16 costs at most the half-width.* Projecting the
pose-source axis's measured half-widths at the same n — the closest available reference, **not a
measurement of this axis**:

| | half-width | upper bound on FP16's share of −1.41 |
|---|---:|---:|
| n=400 | ±1.205 | **at most 86 %** |
| **n=931** | **±0.778** | **at most 55 %** |

**"FP16 accounts for at most 86 % of the deficit" is nearly vacuous** — it excludes almost nothing and
would reproduce the n=30 failure at thirteen times the cost. "At most 55 %" is a statement that can go
in the report. That inverts the usual calculus: on the pose-source axis we wanted to establish a
*sign*, so power was about detection; here the half-width **is** the deliverable.

So: 3.5 hours of otherwise-idle board time for the difference between a usable bound and an
unusable one. **Do not stop it at 400.** If it has to stop for a reason other than this choice, a
partial set is still a usable arm and resumable, as you say.

## 2. The 2.6× is overstated because it compares across two different sets

You compared **~11 clips/min (dev)** against **4.37 clips/min (test)**. Those sets have different
clip lengths: dev is 184,093/918 = **200.5 frames/clip**, test is 203,562/931 = **218.6**. So 9 % of
that ratio is clip length, not precision.

Normalising to frames/s, which is what §2.5k reports anyway:

| comparison | FP16 | FP32 | ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| aggregate to aggregate (§2.5k's 32.0 f/s) | 32.0 f/s | 15.9 f/s | **2.01×** |
| batch to batch (dev batches ran 36.3–38.0) | 36.4 f/s | 15.9 f/s | **2.29×** |
| your figure (clips/min, dev vs test) | — | — | 2.52× |

**Your ETA is unaffected and is right:** 203,562 frames at 15.9 f/s is **3.55 h**. Only the ratio
needs correcting, and only before it gets written into RESULTS as a measured property of the engine.

**Ask: quote 2.0× aggregate-to-aggregate, or ~2.3× batch-to-batch, and say which.**

## 3. That ratio contradicts §3.2, and the gap is worth a line

`REPORT.md` §3.2's per-frame bench is **FP32 33.0 ms against FP16 19.7 ms = 1.68×**. The board is
delivering **2.0–2.3×**. So **FP32 is worse in situ than the bench predicts**, by about a third.

Worth noting rather than resolving, because the sign is the interesting part: staging, rsync and
reclaim passes are roughly constant per clip, so including them should push the observed ratio
*toward* 1.0, not away from it. Something else is making FP32 relatively slower at scale — the larger
engine's working set against the same 8 GB, most plausibly, which would fit §4.3's finding that memory
is the binding constraint. **This arm is incidentally the first FP32 pose measurement at scale**, so it
is the only thing that could have shown this.

Not asking for a diagnosis. Asking that §3.2's 1.68× not be cited as the deployment-scale figure once
this lands, since it understates the cost of the precision choice the report recommends against.
