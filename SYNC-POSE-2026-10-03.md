## 1. The frontier's energy model is composed from the pruned checkpoint, but every end-to-end run uses the full one

This is the important item, because it explains the "~6 % LOW" caveat in `unisign/frontier.py` and it
changes a number in the deliverable.

`frontier.py` composes `pose_J_per_s × clip_seconds + LM_J`, where `LM_J` comes from RESULTS.md §2.9C
— headed **"(pruned checkpoint)"**. But M1 and the whole 2×2 grid run
`weights/openasl_pose_only_slt.pth` with the unpruned `weights/mt5-base`. So the only end-to-end
validation of the frontier is against a *different model* from the one the frontier describes.

**The size of the mismatch, measured.** On the board at 16 fps, greedy → beam 4 costs
**+6.95 J/sentence (+26.9 % of system energy)**. The composition predicts **+1.41 J (+5.1 %)**. It
understates the cost of decoder width by about **5× in absolute joules**.

The mechanism is straightforward: the output projection cost scales with `beams × vocab`, and the full
checkpoint's vocabulary is **250,112 against the pruned 26,078**. §L4 already measured the per-token
consequence — full decoder 69 ms/token vs pruned 55 — but that was latency at one config, so it was
never carried into the energy model.

**Applying a single full/pruned factor of 1.255 to `LM_J` lines the beam-4 cells up almost exactly:**

| cell | measured J | composed (pruned `LM_J`) | composed (×1.255 on `LM_J`) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| M1, source, beam 4 | 50.37 | 47.92 (+5.1 %) | 50.91 (**−1.1 %**) |
| 24 fps, beam 4 | 42.94 | 39.59 (+8.5 %) | 42.39 (**+1.3 %**) |
| 16 fps, beam 4 | 32.75 | 29.31 (+11.7 %) | 31.85 (**+2.8 %**) |
| source, greedy | 43.11 | 45.25 (−4.7 %) | 47.56 (−9.4 %) |
| 16 fps, greedy | 25.80 | 27.90 (−7.5 %) | 30.08 (−14.2 %) |

Note the last two rows: **the flat factor fixes beam 4 and breaks greedy**, which is itself
informative — the penalty should scale with the number of beams, since that is what multiplies the
vocabulary-sized projection. So the correct fix is not a constant.

### What we think should happen, and it is your call

**The deployed system should be the pruned checkpoint** — it is 4× smaller, costs −0.28 BLEU-4
[−0.63, +0.05], and loads in ~1.5 GB instead of needing >5.3 GB (see §3). If that is right, then the
frontier's energy model is the correct one and **our end-to-end runs used the wrong checkpoint**. M1
used the released one because it is the FP32 reference rung, which was right for a first run and
wrong as a basis for validating the frontier.

So the decisive experiment is to **re-run the grid on the pruned checkpoint**. If the composition
residual collapses, the explanation is confirmed and the frontier needs only a line stating which
model it describes. We will run this next unless you object — it is cheap, and it also sidesteps the
memory problem in §3 entirely.

**Until then, please do not quote the beam-width energy cost from the composition.** The +5.1 % figure
is for the pruned model; on the full model we measure +26.9 %. The *accuracy* side of your frontier is
unaffected — it was always measured, never composed.

| 16 fps, greedy | 136 | 1 | 4705.7 ± 121.4 | 3336.7 | 1355.6 | 25.80 | 2.390 |

**What it settles.**

- **The stages are exactly additive within a run** (pose + convert + LM = total to 0.04 ms). So the
  composition residual is not a within-run accounting gap — which is what sent us looking at the
  checkpoint mismatch in §1.
- **Pose is invariant to decoder width** (+2.3 %, inside the run-to-run spread) and **linear in frame
  count** at ~23.7 ms/frame. Both are independence the pipeline is supposed to have.
- **Frame rate is the big knob and decoder width the small one — now on the board, not in a model.**
  Source → 16 fps at greedy: −41.2 % latency, −40.2 % energy. Greedy → beam 4 at 16 fps: +19.3 %
  latency, +26.9 % energy, almost all inside the LM (+61.1 %). Your §3.6/§3.7 conclusion survives
  end-to-end measurement; only the *magnitude* of the beam-width term moves, and it moves against
  beam 4.
- **`mJ_per_frame` is the wrong denominator for the system.** It *rises* under subsampling,
  169.07 → 189.70, because the per-sentence LM cost is spread over fewer frames. End-to-end energy
  must be per sentence. The per-frame basis is still correct for the pose stage alone.
- **Reproducibility is good on the LM and mediocre on pose.** Re-running 16 fps/greedy hours later
  reproduced the LM at **1356.8 vs 1355.6 ms** (0.1 %) and the text exactly, while pose moved
  3648.7 vs 3336.7 ms (+9 %). Treat single-run pose timings as ±10 %.

**n = 1 clip, so no accuracy claim.** Reference: *"When I moved to Texas one year and a half ago, this
was my first artwork."* Decodes: source/greedy "…my first Airbnb subscription"; 24 fps/beam 4 "…my
first priority"; 16 fps/beam 4 "…my first trip"; 16 fps/greedy "I moved to Texas two years ago and it
was my first time." All recover the clause structure, all miss the content word.

## 3. Memory: we had the threshold wrong by 1.3 GB, and it was not a ceiling

RESULTS.md §5.2 said the full checkpoint needs **~4 GB** of MemFree, inferred from M1 succeeding at
4625 MB. **That number does not reproduce.** Measured today, loading the full checkpoint:

| MemFree after reclaim | outcome |
|---:|---|
| 5214 MB | **fails** at `_load_state_dict_into_meta_model` |
| 5268 MB | succeeds |
| 5292–5478 MB | succeeds (the three cells on 10-02) |
| 5726–5768 MB | succeeds |

There is a **cliff just above 5.2 GB**, and `drop_file_cache.py --target-free-mb=5000` reclaims to
only ~5200, so it straddles it. That is why three cells failed at *load* on 10-03 while the identical
16 fps/greedy cell reproduced perfectly at 5268 MB. Target is now **6200** with the guard at **5400**.

**Two corrections to things we told you.**

1. We said `source × beam 4` had hit a real memory ceiling in beam search and that frame subsampling
   was therefore a *feasibility* requirement for beam search. **Retracted.** M1 ran that exact cell at
   *less* free memory. It was the same reclaim-target problem.
2. The surviving general lesson is that **MemFree is not a sufficient readiness check** on this board,
   and the ~4 GB figure in §5.2 was a single success treated as a threshold — the same error shape as
   the occupancy episode, which is uncomfortable but worth saying plainly.

**This is another reason to prefer the pruned checkpoint for deployment**: ~1.5 GB to load against
>5.3 GB, on a board where 5.3 GB requires an explicit page-cache reclaim every single time.
