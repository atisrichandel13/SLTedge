# Complete findings sync — pose track → Atisri, 2026-09-30

**What this is.** Everything the pose track has measured, decided, retracted or broken, in one place.
Written to be read cold, by a person or a model. It supersedes `HANDOFF-LM-TRACK-2026-09-26.md` (same
content, plus everything since) and duplicates `results/RESULTS.md` deliberately so nothing here needs
a second file open.

**How to read the numbers.**
- Every figure states its **metric** and its **denominator**. Most of the confusion in this project has
  come from two numbers that looked comparable and were not; §14 lists the specific traps.
- "**established**" means a 95 % paired bootstrap CI that excludes zero. "**not established**" means the
  direction is *unknown*, not that it is small. Report it that way.
- Everything marked **RETRACTED** or **CORRECTED** is kept in place rather than deleted. §12 is the full
  list of things I got wrong, because several were wrong in the same way and the pattern matters.
- Where a number is inferred rather than measured, it says so.

runs end-to-end on the board at 1.06× real time and does not thermally throttle over 30 minutes;

## 0. CORRECTIONS after reading `lm-track-report-2026-09-30.md` (Atisri)

Her report corrects three of my claims. **All three corrections are right**; this section supersedes the
affected parts below, which are left in place with pointers.

**1. "16 fps is free" is WRONG — superseded.** My §7B said Δ −0.15 BLEU-4, CI [−3.20, +2.89], "no
measured accuracy cost". That was **n=30 with a CI ~6 BLEU-4 wide**. At n=976 (test) and n=967 (dev)
the cost is real:

| 30 → 16 fps | BLEU-4 | ROUGE-L |
|---|---|---|
| test, n=976 | −1.33 [−2.00, −0.64] | **−1.63** |
| dev, n=967 | −0.34 [−0.97, +0.53] | **−1.30** |

**ROUGE-L replicates on both splits; BLEU-4 does not** — a handful of sentences flipping one 4-gram
match is the whole effect size at n≈1000. Her standing rule, which I adopt: **any effect below ~1 BLEU-4
at n≈1000 should be carried by ROUGE-L, with BLEU-4 quoted as unable to resolve it.**

She also showed that at **n=300** the same un-adapted model scored **+1.10 BLEU-4 higher at 16 fps than
at source rate** — the opposite sign of a known effect. My measurement was **n=30**, well inside that
unreliable regime, so both my +1.51 at 24 fps (which I did call noise) and my −0.15 at 16 fps (which I
wrongly called "no cost") are noise. **"Not established" meant unknown, and I then wrote it up as "no
cost" — the exact error §14 of this document warns about.**

**A real bug in my measurement, which she found independently.** OpenASL is **not one frame rate**. In our
931 clips: 76.5 % at ~30 fps, 22.2 % at ~24, 7 clips at 59.94. My `subsample_pkl.py` and
`03_infer_frames.py --keep-fps` hardcode `--src-fps 29.97`, so a 24 fps clip labelled "16 fps" was
actually subsampled to ~12.8 fps. **6 of the 30 clips I measured were off-rate.** Her
`fps_ratio_for_clip` derives each clip's own rate from frames ÷ duration, which is correct. Our tools
need the same fix — logged in §16.

**2. My INT8 justification was wrong.** I argued INT8 was dead because W8A16 keeps activations in fp16
and fp16 breaks mT5. Their implementation is **W8A32** — int8 weights, fp16 *stored* scales cast up
before use, and activations, matmul and accumulation all fp32. The label in guide row 3.2 said W8A16
until 09-28, but I should have checked the implementation rather than reasoning from the name. Her
empirical disproof is cleaner than any argument: **the INT8 config scores 22.79 BLEU-4, which is
impossible under the fp16 failure mode.** My *second* reason — host-bound decoder — was correct and
sufficient alone. **Right decision, wrong justification.** §9.2 below is corrected accordingly.

**3. "Beam width is the frontier" is LM-local, and false of the system.** Per BLEU-4 point:

| lever | system energy | Δ BLEU-4 | **J per BLEU-4 point** |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30 → 24 fps | −16.7 % | −0.07 | **103.5** |
| 30 → 16 fps | −37.9 % | −1.33 | **12.3** |
| beam 4 → beam 2 | −1.8 % | −0.81 | **1.0** |
| beam 4 → greedy | −5.0 % | −1.99 | **1.1** |

Beam width is the LM's lever and the **worst system-level lever**: the decoder is host-bound so beam
count barely moves LM joules, and the LM is only ~25 % of system energy. **Frame rate is 10–100× more
efficient per point.** My §7C framing was right about the LM in isolation and wrong as a system claim.

**Revised operating point — hers, and I agree: beam 4 @ 24 fps.** 17 % less system energy, BLEU-4 −0.07
(tied), ROUGE-L +0.15. Free. **Not 16 fps @ beam 4**, which buys 38 % but costs ~1.3–1.6 ROUGE-L.

## 3. Pose front-end: speed and energy (Task 1, P1, P2)

All `jetson-lpcv-03`, mode 0, 3×299 frames of the baseline clip.

| config | GPU MHz | pre ms | TRT ms | post ms | total ms | avg W | mJ/frame | dyn mJ/frame |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| RTMPose-x 384×288 FP32 — *original baseline* | 612 | 10.7 | 66.7 | 1.5 | **79.2** | 8.20 | 733 | 410 |
| RTMW-l-m 256×192 FP32 | 598 | 6.1 | 25.6 | 1.2 | **33.0** | 6.53 | 275 | 123 |
| **RTMW-l-m 256×192 FP16 — the choice** | **310** | 4.7 | **13.8** | 1.2 | **19.7** | **4.93** | **127** | **34** |
| RTMPose-x FP16 plain — **fails the gate** | 414 | 9.4 | 22.7 | 1.5 | 33.5 | 6.20 | 254 | 106 |
| RTMPose-x FP16 mixed (7/465 layers FP32) | 413 | 10.2 | 23.3 | 1.5 | 35.0 | 6.15 | 264 | 110 |

**Net: 4.0× the frame rate on the compute basis (3.5× end-to-end), and either 5.8× or 12× less energy
per frame depending on which energy you mean** — 5.8× on total mJ/frame (733 → 127), 12× on dynamic
(410 → 34). The gap between those two factors is the fixed ~3.6 W idle draw. **Quote the total figure
as the headline and the dynamic figure as the engine-level result**; a reader who sees 12× without the
qualifier will assume total system energy dropped that much, and it did not.

**FP16 on RTMPose-x is numerically broken and we found out why.** The head's
`/mlp/mlp.0/ReduceSum` peaks at **142,292.8** against the FP16 max of 65,504.
`--fp32-layers 'mlp\.0'` (7 of 465 layers) repairs it for under 3 % latency: simcc 0.649 → 0.023,
within-2 px 67.7 % → 96.1 %. RTMW has **zero** overflowing tensors. Same class of failure as mT5 FP16
on your side (§9) — Jetson FP16 breaks *heads and feed-forwards*, not convolutions.

**Energy, not latency, is the cross-precision metric.** The governor downclocks on lighter work
(612 MHz for RTMPose-x FP32 vs 310 MHz for RTMW FP16), so RTMW's 25.6 → 13.8 ms is a 1.9× wall-clock
gain achieved *at roughly half the clock*. Per-clock the gain is larger; the honest headline is energy.

## 4. Where the frame time goes, and the CPU/GPU power coupling

Prompted by an inconsistency Tushar spotted (a row read 19.7 ms *and* 40 fps, but 1000/19.7 = 51).
Both were real; they were two metrics printed as one. Instrumented the two untimed stages:

| stage | in `total_ms`? | FP16 | FP32 |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| `imread` — JPEG decode from disk | **no** | 5.33 | 6.83 |
| `preprocess` — warp, BGR→RGB, normalise, transpose (CPU) | yes | 4.76 | 6.10 |
| `trt` — input upload + execute + stream sync | yes | 13.66 | 25.83 |
| `postprocess` — simcc D2H 477 KB, argmax, affine back | yes | 1.28 | 1.22 |
| **`total_ms`** | — | **19.71** | **33.15** |
| `collect` — keypoints → Python lists | **no** | 0.16 | 0.16 |
| residual | **no** | 0.01 | 0.01 |
| **wall ms/frame** | — | **25.20** | **40.14** |

**Two fps figures, both correct, never interchangeable:** 51 fps compute (1000/total) vs 40 fps
end-to-end (wall clock, which pays JPEG decode). Prefer compute for design claims — a deployed pipeline
decodes H.264 on NVDEC, so the 5.3 ms is an artifact of reading pre-extracted JPEGs. Both are now
emitted as `fps_compute` and `fps_end_to_end` in every run JSON.

**The finding that matters for your measurements: under the 15 W cap the CPU and GPU compete.**
`imread` and `preprocess` both inflate by **×1.281 — identical to three digits** — between the FP16 and
FP32 runs, on identical input and identical code, while measured `cpu0_MHz` falls 1045 → 897 (×1.165);
DRAM contention with a 604 MHz GPU covers the rest. `postprocess` is flat because it is half a
device→host copy, which gets *faster* at the higher GPU clock.

Consequences:
- **`pre ms` is not a property of the preprocessing code**, it is a property of the configuration's power
  budget. Comparing it across engines measures the governor as much as the code. (This retired an old
  claim that preprocess "does not shrink with GPU quantization" — on RTMW it shrinks 22 %.)
- **Every LM number you have was measured with the board otherwise idle**, so it is an *optimistic*
  bound on what the LM gets in a real pipeline — and your decoder is host-bound (§9), exactly the work
  that suffers when the CPU downclocks.
- `cpu0_MHz` was already in every power CSV; it had simply never been read next to the timings.

## 5. Precision effect on keypoints (P4)

133 keypoints, judged only where the FP32 engine scores the joint ≥ 0.3 (the threshold
`common/pose_to_unisign.py` gates on, so a lower-scoring joint is zeroed before the ST-GCN and its
position cannot reach the translation). `unisign_used` = the **69 of 133** keypoints the pose-only path
consumes. Euclidean distance.

Baseline clip, 299 frames:

| config | group | n | ≤1 px | ≤2 px | ≤5 px | p99.9 | max |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| RTMW FP16 | hands | 11603 | 78.0 % | 78.0 % | 99.2 % | 31.3 | 134.4 |
| | face | 20332 | 91.9 % | 91.9 % | 100.0 % | 3.1 | 3.1 |
| | unisign_used | 19609 | 82.7 % | 82.7 % | 99.5 % | 17.3 | 134.4 |
| RTMPose-x FP16 **plain** | face | 20332 | 41.0 % | 49.3 % | **49.6 %** | 500.8 | 507.2 |
| RTMPose-x FP16 mixed | face | 20332 | 86.2 % | 99.5 % | 100.0 % | 2.0 | 2.1 |
| | unisign_used | 18722 | 48.3 % | 81.7 % | 99.3 % | 14.9 | 108.4 |

Five signers, 1510 frames: **99.81 %** (RTMW FP16) and **99.75 %** (RTMPose-x FP16 mixed) of consumed
keypoints within 5 px of FP32 — better than the single clip.

**simcc bins, and why pixel thresholds mislead.** Coordinates are an argmax over discrete bins, so every
error is a whole multiple of one bin, and **bin width is not constant**:

```
bin_px = max(crop_w, crop_h × 0.75) × 1.25 / (input_w × simcc_split_ratio)
```

It scales with the signer bbox: measured 1.71–2.45 px for RTMW on native crops, 1.14–1.63 px for
RTMPose-x, 0.72 px for RTMW on a 224 crop. So the ≤1 px and ≤2 px columns are **measuring bbox geometry**
— a 13-point swing across clips — and only ≤5 px is comparable. **Any agreement threshold must be stated
in bins, not pixels.** The sharpest illustration: the same engine pair reads 99.81 % within 5 px on a
native crop and 99.99 % on a 224 crop, implying the small crop is *safer* — which inverts the moment you
express 5 px as ~2 bins versus ~7 bins.

Expressed in bins, one ordering reproduces on two independent crops: RTMW agrees with its own FP32
better than RTMPose-x mixed does — ≤2 bins **99.39 % vs 96.58 %** (native) and **99.76 % vs 98.72 %**
(224), worst case 53 vs 147 bins and 44 vs 77 bins.

### 6.3 Why our poses differed: a 1.68× coordinate-frame mismatch

Diffed our poses against theirs in the units the frozen encoder consumes — 9 body joints **absolute**,
21+21 hands **wrist-relative**, 18 face **nose-tip-relative**, score < 0.3 zeroed:

| group | frame | median difference |
|---|---|---:|
| body | **absolute** | **0.1175** |
| left hand | wrist-relative | 0.0509 |
| right hand | wrist-relative | 0.0572 |
| face | nose-tip-relative | 0.0298 |

Body is the only absolute group and the worst by 2–4× — the signature of a **global scale error**, which
cancels for root-relative groups.

**Cause.** They normalise over a **square**: bbox expanded on its *short* side, black-padded where it
leaves the frame, resized to 224. We normalise over the bbox crop. Signer boxes are tall and narrow, so
their square side is much wider than our crop width and the signer fills less of their frame. Predicted
`square_side / our_crop_width` against measured shoulder-width ratio over 30 clips: **correlation 0.968**,
mean abs error 0.028 (predicted 1.657 ± 0.070, measured 1.679 ± 0.083).

**Fix, exact arithmetic, no re-extraction** (`common/renorm_to_openasl.py`):

| | shoulder ratio ours/theirs | body median difference |
|---|---:|---:|
| our crop frame | 1.679 ± 0.083 | 0.1328 |
| **their square frame** | **1.014 ± 0.014** | **0.0055** |

A **24× reduction**. **Ruled out along the way:** confidence gating (we zero 2.8 % of consumed joints,
they zero 2.8 %, every group within 0.1 pp, whole-hand gating 8.3 % vs 8.3 %) and a translation component
(a per-clip mean shift explains 2 % of the difference). The residual 0.03–0.05 in root-relative units is
genuine detector disagreement, which no transform removes because our detector ran on different pixels —
our native 502–754 px crops versus their 224 square with 15–50 % black padding.

**Deployment consequence:** the transform must live in `common/pose_to_unisign.py` on the live path, or
the demo reproduces the bug the offline numbers corrected.

### 9.1 Your results, now with confidence intervals

**Vocabulary pruning is free and the decode knobs are not.** Report pruning as "not established as a
loss" rather than "−0.28 BLEU-4", which reads like a measured penalty. Vocabulary: 26,078 of 250,112
tokens kept; 587.7 M → 243.6 M params; 1187 → 571 MB checkpoint.

**This puts a number on the TensorRT decision:** the TRT path is greedy-only, and greedy costs **2.00
BLEU-4**. So "PyTorch encoder + TRT greedy decoder", the fastest configuration measured, buys a 2.9×
decoder speedup at a measured cost of 2 BLEU-4 — roughly a third of the entire n=5 pose-front-end gap,
spent on the decoder alone. Beam 2 on PyTorch costs 0.81 instead.

### 9.2 INT8 is dropped (Tushar's call, 2026-09-28)

**Guide row 3.2 is marked ❌.** The premise does not hold:

| engine set | tokens vs PyTorch | max \|Δlogprob\| | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| FP32 | **identical** | 5.6e-4 | passes |
| FP16 | **broken**: 64× token 0 | 10.2 (uniform) | unusable |
| BF16 | diverges at step 3 (22 vs 24 tokens) | 1.1 | unusable |

The FP16 failure is diagnostic, not random: every step returns token 0 with logprob −10.169, exactly
−ln(26078) — a uniform distribution over the pruned vocabulary, i.e. saturated logits. This is the
documented T5/mT5 FP16 overflow. **W8A16 keeps activations in FP16 and inherits it.**

Independently, **INT8 would buy memory rather than latency**: the FP16 decoder step was measured *no
faster* than FP32 (18 vs 19 ms/token) while the FP16 encoder was 3–4× faster. That asymmetry says the
step is **host-bound, not GEMM-bound** — the 19 ms is dominated by re-feeding 50 input bindings and
cloning outputs per step, not by the 238 M-param matmuls. Quantizing weights does not make host-side
Python faster.

**And the frontier does not need it** (§7C). If it is ever revisited, the only credible variant is INT8
weights with **FP32** activations (PyTorch dynamic quant).

### 9.3 Beam search does not exist on the TensorRT path

`unisign/trt_decode.py` hardcodes `num_beams=1` (line 79), for the reference too. Beam through the
explicit-KV-cache engines needs per-beam cache reordering — an implementation task, not a flag.
`unisign/unisign_infer.py` does take `--num-beams`, so beam 2/4 exist on the PyTorch path.

Awkwardly, **the TRT encoder is slower than PyTorch** (82 vs 57 ms): with TF32 off TensorRT falls back
to plain FP32 FFMA kernels while cuBLAS has better FP32 GEMM tiles. So the fastest configuration is
**PyTorch encoder + TRT greedy decoder** — which by construction cannot do beam search. Decoder alone is
**2.9× faster than PyTorch** (19 vs 55 ms/token). If the decoder needs to be cheaper the lever is the
step loop (CUDA graphs, device-resident KV cache), not the input length.

### 9.4 Memory figures, and which metric each one is

This caused a real misreading. **All three are the pruned model.** The TensorRT engines were built from
`models/mt5_pruned_onnx/`, which is the pruned export.

| figure | metric | source |
|---|---|---|
| **2.41 GB** | torch **peak-allocated**, *full* 582 M model | L4 |
| **1.03 GB** | torch **peak-allocated**, *pruned* 238 M model | L4 |
| **2.07 GB** | **whole-process resident** (MemFree delta), pruned TRT engines | L8.2 |

2.07 > 1.03 for two reasons at once, neither being "the engine wasn't pruned": a different metric
(process-resident vs torch peak-allocated), **and** the 3-graph export duplicates decoder weights
(`decoder_init.onnx` 616 MB + `decoder_step.onnx` 558 MB both contain the decoder). So "pruned + TRT
FP32" *has* been measured as a combination — 19.2 ms/token, identical tokens to PyTorch.

**When comparing TRT to PyTorch use the *pruned* PyTorch row: 19 vs 55 ms/token, a 2.9× decoder speedup.**
Comparing 19 against the full model's 69 gives a wrong 3.6×.

## 10. The LM load memory ceiling — new, unresolved, and it affects your track

The end-to-end sustained run keeps failing with
`RuntimeError: NVML_SUCCESS == r INTERNAL ASSERT FAILED ... CUDACachingAllocator.cpp:1017`.

**First, read that assert as "out of memory".** Every occurrence in this project is preceded by
`NvMapMemAllocInternalTagged ... error 12` (ENOMEM). nvmap fails, PyTorch tries to build an informative
OOM message, NVML is only partly supported on Jetson, and the assert replaces the real error. **It is not
a PyTorch bug.** This retroactively explains five evals lost on 09-26 as one cause, not two.

**Four probes located it, and it is not where I assumed:**

| probe | MemFree at start | result |
|---|---:|---|
| TRT engine alone | 3988 MB | ✅ works (uses 242 MB) |
| **LM alone** | 3863 MB | ❌ **fails**, drops to 186 MB |
| LM alone, after maximum reclaim | 4641 MB | ❌ fails |
| LM→TRT and TRT→LM | ~4000 MB | ❌ both fail |

**It is not TensorRT/PyTorch coexistence and not the pose engine. The LM load alone exhausts the board.**

**Mechanism:** `load_model` with a pruned checkpoint materialises the **full 250k-vocab** mT5, slices it
to 26k, then copies to device. On unified memory that peaks near **4.2 GB** (2.3 full + 0.95 sliced +
0.95 device copy) — **more than loading the full checkpoint costs** — while `MemAvailable` caps around
5.3 GB. That is why identical configurations succeed at 4932 MB and fail at 4689.

**Consequence you should know: M1's single success sits within ~10 % of the board's ceiling.** It is not
a robust configuration and should not be reported as one.

**Fix, implemented but UNVERIFIED** (commit `5ee64df`, `unisign/model.py` — *your file*): use a
pre-pruned mT5 directory directly when its `config.json` `vocab_size` matches the checkpoint's
`keep_ids`, skipping the materialisation. Peak should drop from ~4.2 GB to ~1.2 GB.

**It needs two halves, and the second is a trap.** `prune_and_save` writes a **checkpoint file**, not an
HF directory, and `PrunedTokenizer` is a **runtime wrapper with no `save_pretrained`**. So a pre-pruned
directory carries pruned **weights** beside the **original 250k tokenizer** — loading it without
remapping ids would produce **fluent but wrong text, with no error**. Hence
`attach_pruned_tokenizer()`, which does the non-weight half of `prune_vocab` and **refuses to run if the
embedding row count does not match `keep_ids`**, so a wrong assumption fails loudly instead of
mistranslating.

**What I need from you on this:** confirmation that `weights/mt5-base-openasl-pruned` is a
`save_pretrained` dump at vocab 26,078 and what tokenizer files it contains. I could not check — the
board has been unreachable since 09-28 and `weights/` exists only there. **No result should be taken
from this path until it reproduces a known-good sentence** (M1's output above, or any eval prediction).

## 13. Process and infrastructure findings (the traps)

- **`posix_fadvise` is not enough on a shared board.** With another user's desktop session active,
  MemFree sat at 100 MB with 6.2 GB cached and the cache-drop freed 14 MB. `drop_file_cache.py
  --target-free-mb=N` now forces kernel reclaim by briefly allocating anonymous memory. **Size it to the
  target, not the deficit** — a deficit-sized allocation fits in already-free memory and evicts nothing.
- **Two PyTorch processes on this board kill each other.** Launching a second eval behind a `pgrep` guard
  that did not hold killed both. Everything now runs strictly sequentially in one process.
- **Board-generated summaries need verifying, not trusting — three for three.** (1) A heredoc into
  `run.sh exec-batch` arrives as EOF because stdin is not forwarded, producing a silently empty report.
  (2) Same again. (3) The board had a stale Sep-18 `bootstrap_ci.py` without `--out`, so argparse errored
  and nothing matched the grep. **Call python by file path, and check the file you are running is current.**
- **Stale duplicates in the board repo root.** Four Sep-18 copies of project files, left by earlier flat
  rsyncs, had diverged from the real ones. Deleted. `jrsync.exp` needs `--` before rsync flags.
- **A resumable job is not a hang-proof job.** The full-split fetch hung for **six hours** at 397 clips
  after a laptop sleep — process alive, no progress, so the resume logic never ran. Root cause:
  `subprocess.run(timeout=)` kills only the direct child, and yt-dlp's ffmpeg grandchild inherited the
  pipes, so `communicate()` blocked again *after* the timeout fired. Fixed with `start_new_session` +
  `killpg`, yt-dlp `--socket-timeout/--retries`, and a supervisor that restarts on a 10-minute stall.
- **The per-clip container overhead was 9 s of 15.4 s** — docker exec + engine load + CUDA init. At 974
  clips that is 2.4 h of pure waste per config. `09_batch_clips.py` builds the engine once.
- **nvmap allocates only from MemFree and never reclaims page cache.** Drop caches before loading *any*
  large model, not just before TensorRT builds.
- **zsh does not word-split `set -- $pair`**; `.gitignore` does not accept trailing `#` comments. Both
  cost time.
