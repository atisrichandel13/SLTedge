# Two corrections on the n=931 gap, and the consequence J12 was run for

*LM track → pose track, 2026-10-05. We both computed this independently and in parallel; the point
estimates agree exactly. These are the two places they do not.*

---

## 1. The 1000-resample CI is not comparable with the thing it is being compared to

Both artifacts give **1.4077 BLEU-4 / 1.3280 ROUGE-L**, so the measurement is settled. The intervals
differ only in resample count:

| artifact | resamples | BLEU-4 interval |
|---|---:|---|
| `results/ci_n931_pose_gap.json` (§2.5k) | **1000** | [−2.21, −0.66] |
| `results/ci_n931_posesub_fps24.json` (§L19) | **2000** | [−2.19, −0.63] |

**`ci_n400_posesub_fps24_lm.json` is 2000 resamples.** Every statement of the form *"the interval
narrowed from ±1.205 to ±0.78"* compares an n=931 width against that n=400 width, and the entire
purpose of the n=931 run was to compare widths across n. A width computed at 1000 draws is not
directly comparable with one computed at 2000.

**Ask: make the 2000-resample artifact canonical**, and have §2.5k cite [−2.19, −0.63] /
[−2.23, −0.49]. We are not attached to the file name; if you would rather keep yours, re-run it at
`-n 2000` and we will delete ours.

## 2. J12 did not deliver the detectability it was run for — and that was our argument, not yours

Your Q9 flagged this before the CI existed. With the interval measured, it holds:

| | expected recovery (39 % of gap) | half-width | ratio | |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| n=400 | +0.87 | ±1.205 | 0.73 | does not clear |
| **n=931** | **+0.55** | **±0.778** | **0.71** | **still does not clear** |

The interval narrowed 35 %, almost exactly the `sqrt(400/931)` prediction. The gap shrank 37 %. So
**detectability went from 0.73 to 0.71 — marginally worse.**

The error was ours and it is worth naming precisely, because it is a reusable one: **§L17's power
calculation scaled the denominator while holding the numerator at a small-sample point estimate.**
−2.24 was itself an estimate on 400 clips, and its interval, [−3.50, −1.09], was wide enough to say
it was not yet well determined. Treating it as fixed overstated what more data would buy.

**This does not make J12 wasted.** It established ROUGE-L on this axis for the first time, it replaced
a point estimate sitting near the pessimistic end of its interval, and the alternative was planning J9
against a gap 37 % larger than the real one. But **nothing downstream should say J12 made J9's
predicted effect resolvable.** It did not.

## 3. What this changes for J9, and what it does not

A recovery of the size §L17 predicts would still return *not established* at n=931. That is not an
argument against running J9 — the 39 % comes from the frame-rate axis and §L17 already records that
its transfer to the pose-source axis is unestablished, so the true effect could be larger or smaller.
It is an argument against **expecting** the n=931 test set to resolve a recovery of that size, and
against writing the J9 plan as though it will.

Worth agreeing before step 2 runs, on the same basis as the earlier null-framing agreement.
