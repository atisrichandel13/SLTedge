## 3. Decisions taken, and the reason for each

1. **RTMW-l-m 256×192 FP16 is the pose front-end**, not RTMPose-x. 2.6× faster and 2.7× cheaper per
   frame at FP32 already, and unlike RTMPose-x it has no FP16 overflow to work around.
2. **The LM stays FP32 on the board.** FP16 mT5 produces uniform logits (overflow), BF16 has the
   range but its 8-bit mantissa breaks token-exact greedy decoding at step 3. Reduced precision also
   bought no speed, because the decoder is launch-bound, not compute-bound.
3. **TF32 is explicitly cleared for every FP32 baseline.** TensorRT enables it by default on Ampere,
   so an "FP32" engine built with defaults is not FP32 — it failed the correctness gate at 1.55e-3
   and passed at 7.2e-6 once cleared. Any FP32 reference number taken before this is wrong.
4. **Energy per frame, not a latency ratio, is the cross-precision comparison.** The GPU governor
   downclocks with lighter work: busy-sample mean is 612 MHz for RTMPose-x FP32 but 310 MHz for RTMW
   FP16. Lighter engines therefore run at lower clocks, and latency ratios understate the real gain.
5. **The max-pixel gate is demoted; the agreement distribution is the accuracy axis.** Detail in §4.
6. **The gate threshold was not loosened to match.** Raising it from 2 px to 7 px would have turned
   three FAILs into passes without new evidence. Loosening a criterion to accommodate the numbers you
   already have is how a project ratifies its own regression.
7. **P3 crops differ deliberately from OpenASL's own `prep/crop_video.py`.** They square, black-pad
   and resize to 224; we scale the released bbox to the frame and clamp, keeping native resolution,
   because the pose models apply their own affine and padding changes what they see. Documented in the
   script header — changing it later silently breaks comparability with every row already measured.
8. **Vocabulary pruning is accepted**: −59 % params and −52 % checkpoint for a cost bounded at
   ~0.6 BLEU-4 with no retraining.
9. **Any claimed effect under ~1 BLEU needs a bootstrap CI**, because the reproduction noise band
   alone is 0.6 BLEU-4.
10. **Board hygiene is fixed policy**: never sudo, 15 W only, clocks logged never pinned, one engine
    per process, `--workspace-gb 1`, `jetson/drop_file_cache.py` before every build or engine load,
    and results pulled only with `jetson/pull_results.sh`.

## 4. Problems encountered

### Solved, with the finding worth keeping

**TF32 masquerading as FP32.** See decision 3. The kind of bug that silently invalidates a baseline
rather than crashing.

**RTMPose-x FP16 was catastrophically broken — 383 px worst error, 40 % of keypoints usable.** Root
cause found by writing `08_fp16_range_scan.py`, which exposes every intermediate tensor of the ONNX
and compares peaks against FP16's 65504 ceiling: the head's ScaleNorm `ReduceSum` peaks at
**142,293**, 2.2× over the limit. One tensor in 376 saturates and takes the whole head down. The
same scan over RTMW's 398 tensors finds zero overflowing tensors, which is exactly why its FP16
engine is fine. Fixed by pinning the seven `mlp.0` layers to FP32 (`--fp32-layers 'mlp\.0'`), for
under 3 % latency — a new capability in `common/trt_runner.py`.

**The max-px gate could not distinguish a rounding artefact from a catastrophe.** `06_compare_trt.py`
gated on the single worst per-keypoint pixel error over 20 frames. But a simcc head decodes by argmax
over bins, so a flat heatmap on an occluded hand flips a whole bin under 1e-2 of noise — a discrete
decision changing on a tie, not numerical failure. Consequences: the metric *worsens* as you measure
more frames (RTMW FP16's worst went 6.50 px over 20 frames to 134 px over 299); the 2 px threshold
sat *below* RTMW's own 2.17 px error quantum, so it could not pass unless bit-exact; and all three
FP16 engines returned the same FAIL, covering two deployable engines and one genuine catastrophe.
Replaced by `07_kpt_agreement.py` (P4). The gate is kept as a numerical-regression tripwire for FP32
builds and NaN detection, where a max is the correct instrument.

**8 GB board, no root, and nvmap will not reclaim page cache.** Engine builds and loads failed with
CUDA OOM while `free` showed 3–4 GB of cache. `jetson/drop_file_cache.py` evicts our own files with
`posix_fadvise(DONTNEED)`, which needs no privileges.

**Two boards disagreed by 50 % on the same engine** (66.7 ms vs 44.4 ms, 8.2 W vs 11.6 W). The CSV
showed our GPU pinned at 612 MHz whenever busy — the 15 W cap. Scaling by 612/918 reproduces the
other number to 0.1 ms, so the earlier row was taken at the 25 W cap. `/var/lib/nvpmodel/status` can
be stale, so clocks are now sampled during the run rather than trusted from a file.

**OpenASL ships no video.** It is distribution-by-reference: YouTube ids, time ranges, signer bboxes
and sentences. Also undocumented: the label files are gzipped pickles, and bboxes can fall outside
[0,1]. `data/openasl_fetch.py` handles all of it.

**yt-dlp returned HTTP 403 on every section download.** The installed 2026.03.03 build hands ffmpeg a
URL bound to the player client yt-dlp itself used, and ffmpeg's own request is rejected. The first fix
failed because the system `python3` is 3.9, which pins yt-dlp to an older release; rebuilding the venv
on Python 3.13 gives 2026.08.19, which works with no cookies and no sign-in. The `yt-crawl`
environment was left untouched.

**WireGuard and Claude access were mutually exclusive.** The lab tunnel is a full tunnel
(`AllowedIPs = 0.0.0.0/0`), so raising it moves the default route into the lab and kills this
session's own connection. Sockets opened beforehand survive, because macOS caches the route on the
socket — which is why the two appeared compatible when started in the right order. Fix is a split
tunnel: `AllowedIPs = 192.168.1.0/24`, no `DNS =` line, since we address the board by IP with
`HostKeyAlias`. **Not yet applied — this is the current blocker on all board work.**

### Open, unresolved

- **No BLEU for any pose configuration.** Every accuracy claim on the pose side is engine-vs-its-own-FP32,
  which measures precision damage and nothing else. The pose→BLEU path (2.5) is what turns it into a
  translation-quality claim.
- **P4 covers one signer.** 36 k keypoints, all the same person under the same lighting.
- **Hands are the accuracy floor and precision-independent.** Hand agreement is identical in the
  broken and repaired RTMPose-x engines (98.87 % vs 98.93 %), so the residual is argmax instability on
  low-confidence joints, not FP16. Nothing done so far touches it, and hands carry most of the sign
  lexicon. Expect part of the BLEU spread across pose configs to be this noise.
- **The results protocol (C9) has not been applied retroactively.** It requires 3 runs mean ± std,
  ≥3 clips and ≥3 signers, and one 30-minute sustained run for thermal behaviour. Existing rows are
  3×299 frames of a single clip. The P3 data now makes the multi-signer part possible; the sustained
  run has not been done at all.
- **C6 / M1, first on-device end-to-end translation, does not exist yet.** It is the Stage-0 baseline
  every optimization is compared against, and the week-4 gate depends on it.
- **The board keeps no engines between sessions** (shared scratch), so every session pays the rebuild
  cost: 2–10 minutes per engine.
