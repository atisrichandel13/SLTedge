# The 2×2 is built on a superseded operating point

*Revised 2026-10-03 after reading `jetson/e2e_2x2.sh` and RESULTS.md §5.2. The original version of
this file asked for a single corner at 24 fps; your 2×2 is a better design and that part is
withdrawn. What follows is the one thing it still misses.*

## What's wrong

`jetson/e2e_2x2.sh` runs `{source, 16 fps} × {beam 4, greedy}`. The 16 fps row comes from our
priority-1 ask, which was written before the frontier existed. **That priority is out of date.**

The frontier (`results/frontier.png`, RESULTS.md §L16, committed 2026-09-30) puts the recommended
operating point at **beam 4 @ 24 fps**, not at 16 fps:

| cell | system J | BLEU-4 | Δ vs reference |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| beam 4 @ source | 43.4 | 22.87 | reference |
| **beam 4 @ 24 fps** | **36.1** | **22.80** | **−17 % J for −0.07 BLEU-4, CI [−0.53, +0.39]** |
| beam 4 @ 16 fps | 27.0 | 21.54 | −41 % J for −1.33, CI [−2.00, −0.64] |

24 fps is free within noise. **16 fps is an established accuracy loss** — the CI excludes zero. We
are not recommending 16 fps, so the 2×2 as written measures the residual at one cell we recommend
(source) and one we don't (16 fps), and never at the cell we do.

## The ask

**Add 24 fps as a third rate**, making it 3×2 — or if board time is short, just the one cell
`24 fps × beam 4`. Your `--keep-fps` already supports it, so this is a loop bound, not new code.

Your attribution argument is why this is worth doing rather than just taking the 16 fps point: with
source / 24 / 16 you get three frame counts against the same clip and decoder, which constrains the
per-frame term far better than two, and 24 fps is where we need the answer to be right.

## Dropped from this ask

* **`cpu0_MHz`** — withdrawn, you're right that it needed no work. `power_logger` already samples it
  into `aux_avg` and M1's file carries 866.76 MHz, below the 897 MHz downclock point.
* **The single-corner request** — your 2×2 supersedes it for the reason you gave: one more point
  cannot say which factor is mismodelled.

## Unrelated, still outstanding

**We have never produced a board-measured BLEU.** Every accuracy number we quote is offline on the
Mac at n=976; the only board↔offline bridge is the 30-clip batch-size-1 agreement in §5.1. A ~100-clip
end-to-end board pass at the recommended cell would close that, at roughly 15 minutes of board time.
Lower priority than the 24 fps cell above.

## One correction accepted, with thanks

RESULTS.md §5.2: we have **restored** the "memory is not binding at 2.577 GB peak" line in
`LM-TRACK-REPORT-2026-09-30.md` §4 and **un-reopened INT8**, as you asked. The report now records
the full history rather than quietly reverting, because the failure mode is worth keeping: we
adopted a cross-track measurement and retracted a correct finding of our own without asking what
conditions it was taken under.

## Added 2026-10-03, after §5.3: the 2×2 cannot split the residual, and one field would fix it

Your additivity result settles something we had wrong. `unisign/frontier.py` attributed the ~6 %
composition residual to unmodelled within-run stages — the convert step, the idle floor during model
load, the warm-up sentence. **All three are now refuted**, and your data is what refutes them:

* Stages are exactly additive (±0.04 ms), so there is no within-run accounting gap.
* Priced at M1's own 5.56 W average, the 25.7 ms convert step is **0.143 J — 4.5 % of the 3.16 J
  residual.**
* Load and warm-up are outside the power window by construction (§5.1).

The docstring is corrected. The residual therefore comes from comparing **across** runs:
`POSE_J_PER_S` and `LM_J` were measured in separate runs, on a different clip from M1's, each with
its own baseline draw and thermal state.

**What's missing to go further.** §5.3 reports per-stage **latency** (`pose_ms`, `convert_ms`,
`lm_ms`) but only a per-sentence **energy** total. So the residual cannot be split between the pose
term and the LM term — and those are the two terms the composition is built from. Dividing total
joules by the latency shares does not work, because the stages do not draw equal power: the pose
stage is TRT/GPU-bound and the decoder is host-bound, which is exactly the asymmetry §C established.

**The ask: log per-stage energy in the 24 fps × beam 4 cell**, by closing a power window around each
stage rather than around the sentence. Then the composition can be checked term by term instead of
in aggregate, and we would learn whether the ~6 % sits in the pose rate, the LM rate, or the
cross-run baseline.

If that is awkward to retrofit, the fallback is cheaper and still useful: record `baseline_watts`
and the board's thermal state for the 24 fps cell, so it can at least be compared against the runs
`POSE_J_PER_S` and `LM_J` came from.
